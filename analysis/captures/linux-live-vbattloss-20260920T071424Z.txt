What happened, and it is NOT what a USB unplug does:
  * The adapter stayed enumerated: lsusb still listed 18e1:0104 and /dev/ttyACM0 remained.
  * The frame count froze at 35377 and never moved again.
  * No error was raised. The bridge ran on for over two minutes reporting zero new frames,
    with no eVbattLoss indication reaching the read path and no nonzero exit.
  * SIGTERM still shut it down cleanly ('stopped:' line, exit 0).

So to the driver, losing vehicle power is indistinguishable from a silent bus. There is no
asynchronous notification. What DOES distinguish them is READ_VBATT, read after the bridge
was stopped and with the OBD plug still out:

  Open result=0                 (the device still opens with no vehicle power)
  Ioctl READ_VBATT result=0     vbatt_mv=0
  Ioctl READ_VBATT result=0     vbatt_mv=0

against vbatt_mv=13800 earlier the same session with the plug in the car. A program that
wants to tell 'vehicle power gone' from 'quiet bus' should poll READ_VBATT; zero means the
connector lost power. This is the practical answer to the eVbattLoss question: the adapter
does not push the event, but it reports the voltage truthfully when asked.
